Loose Ends

Nov 16, 2026

In January Estonia passed a gambling tax amendment that left out one word and accidentally made online casinos tax-free for a year. Luukas Ilves (their former government CIO) gave the amendment and the Act it changed to a couple of LLMs, and both spotted the gap straight away. Within a day he’d built Apsakaleidja (“mistake finder”), which checks every bill going through their parliament.

Docref seemed like a good place to try that for New Zealand. Every provision has a stable address, so “section 73 of the Electricity Industry Act 2010” can be resolved to an actual block of text and checked.

I asked Claude to run five simple detectors over the 70 Acts and regulations on docref. They look for references to repealed or non-existent sections, mentions of Acts the corpus says are repealed, borrowed definitions that point at the wrong section, and drift between versions. The detectors flagged 433 suspects. Claude then read each one and sorted it into real error, cured by the Legislation Act’s “references to repealed legislation” rule, deliberate transitional wording, or noise. 13 held up, ranked by confidence. Each one can be walked through from the citing provision to what it actually points at, with links to the pinpoint on docref.

My favourites:

  • the Gas Act still gives powers to an “Energy Commission” that lives in a subpart that isn’t there
  • the Regulatory Standards Act 2025 overrides a Public Service Act section that was repealed about six months after it passed

Most of these are loose ends rather than true contradictions, but they’re objective, and someone who knows the area can check each one in a few minutes. That seems like the right first test of whether this approach finds real things. The next step would be getting an expert to check them, then aiming the same pairwise review at harder cases where two live provisions actually conflict.

Contentious Acts

Nov 16, 2026

The overview of changes was interesting, but words changed isn’t a reliable signal of impact of those changes. Thinking about ways to measure or reveal that in the dataset I got to wondering whether we could detect contention around an act - perhaps the signal would be changes and reversions by successive governments, or intense editing of the same stretches of legislation.

This prototype animates the changes over time fo the 3 most contentious looking acts.

Mapping Amendments

Nov 15, 2026

After seeing the version history for acts in legislation.docref.nz, I wondered what the edits looked like overall - where were edits being made, and by who at different times.

I asked Claude to draw this up, with time-blocks coloured by the party in power at the time the change was enacted. I also asked bigger changes to be shown with bigger dots to give a bit of a heat map. I also wanted to be able to dive from the map into the specific changes, so asked for there to be a links through to the docref pages where I could read more.
