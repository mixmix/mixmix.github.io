    Method

    In January 2026 an amendment to Estonia's gambling tax omitted one word, which left online casinos untaxed for the year. Luukas Ilves, Estonia's former government CIO, gave the amendment and the Act it changed to two language models. Both found the gap. He then built Apsakaleidja ("mistake finder") to scan every bill before parliament. His next step was to check bills against existing law.

    This experiment takes that next step for New Zealand, using docref's block-addressed corpus. Simple detectors over-flag pairs of provisions that point at each other. A reviewer then reads each pair and sorts it into genuine error, cured by Legislation Act 2019 s 38, intentional transitional wording, or false positive.

    Most findings are dangling references, not head-on conflicts between two live rules. They are objective, and an expert can check each one in minutes. That makes them a good first test of whether this method finds real problems.

    Read every finding as a lead, not a legal opinion. Legislation Act s 38(2) reads a reference to repealed legislation as a reference to whatever replaced it, so several findings are untidy rather than legally broken. Parliamentary Counsel can fix those as editorial changes under ss 86–87. All text is docref's current consolidation. Confirm against legislation.govt.nz before citing.
